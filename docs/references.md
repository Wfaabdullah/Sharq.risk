# SHARQ.RISK professional references

Access date for links in this library: 21 November 2026. Titles, issuing organizations, versions, and official URLs were checked against the issuing organizations' pages or publications. The source library provides professional, accounting, supervisory, educational, and market context. It does not establish SHARQ.RISK compliance, endorsement, actuarial validation, or formula correctness.

## Supervisory and accounting references

1. **Insurance Core Principles and Common Framework for the Supervision of Internationally Active Insurance Groups — ICP 14: Valuation.** International Association of Insurance Supervisors (IAIS). December 2024 compilation; revised ICP 14 adopted 5 December 2024. [Official IAIS publication PDF](https://www.iais.org/uploads/2025/06/IAIS-ICPs-and-ComFrame-December-2024.pdf). The source addresses supervisory principles for valuing insurer assets and liabilities for solvency purposes. It is a reference for future valuation and solvency workflows; it does not validate this application's formulas, code, mappings, or output.

2. **IFRS 17 Insurance Contracts.** International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) / IFRS Foundation. Issued May 2017; amendments issued June 2020; the IFRS Foundation overview currently labels the standard “Standard 2026 Issued.” [Official IFRS Foundation standard overview](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-17-insurance-contracts/). Relevant for insurance-contract accounting terminology and context. The application is not an IFRS 17 reporting tool, and the standard does not validate its simplified premium or ratio formulas. Contract-specific accounting judgments and records are outside this prototype.

## Actuarial data and professional references

3. **Actuarial Standard of Practice No. 23 — Data Quality.** Actuarial Standards Board (ASB). Revised edition adopted December 2016, effective 30 April 2017, Doc. No. 185. [Official ASB standard page](https://www.actuarialstandardsboard.org/asops/data-quality/) and [official PDF](https://www.actuarialstandardsboard.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/asop023_185.pdf). Provides guidance for actuaries selecting, reviewing, using, and relying on data and communicating significant limitations; it does not require a data audit. It is context for the data-quality workflow and is not evidence that SHARQ.RISK complies with the standard or that its formulas are validated.

4. **Statement of Principles Regarding Property and Casualty Insurance Ratemaking.** Casualty Actuarial Society (CAS). Adopted May 1988, rescinded December 2020, and reinstated May 2021 for reference for U.S.-regulated ratemaking. [Official CAS publication page](https://www.casact.org/statement-principles-regarding-property-and-casualty-insurance-ratemaking) and [CAS-hosted PDF](https://www.casact.org/sites/default/files/2021-06/Statement%20of%20Principles%20Regarding%20P%26C%20Casualty%20Insurance%20Ratemaking_2021.pdf). A conceptual reference discussing prospective ratemaking considerations. It is not practice guidance and does not prescribe or validate the formulas implemented in this dashboard.

5. **Risk and Insurance.** Education and Examination Committee of the Society of Actuaries (SOA), P-21-05, Second Printing; publication date not stated in the verified PDF. [Official SOA study note PDF](https://www.soa.org/globalassets/assets/files/edu/p-21-05.pdf). Educational material describing frequency and severity concepts and their combination into an overall loss distribution. The note says it does not represent official SOA or committee opinion, interpretation, or endorsement. It does not validate SHARQ.RISK's implementation.

## Official Saudi market context

6. **The Saudi Insurance Market Report 2025.** Insurance Authority (Saudi Arabia), 2025 edition; publication date not stated. [Official report PDF](https://www.ia.gov.sa/SectorReports/The%20Saudi%20Insurance%20Market%20Report%202025.pdf) and [Insurance Authority annual publications index](https://www.ia.gov.sa/en/information-center/reports/annual-publications). The report provides official sector-level context for the Saudi insurance market. It is not used as a data source for the synthetic demonstration portfolio. Future comparisons should align definitions, periods, and populations before comparing figures. The report does not validate SHARQ.RISK formulas or implementation.

## KPI source discipline

The KPI method cards document SHARQ.RISK's own demonstration conventions. Where a source is linked to a KPI, the card explains whether it is accounting, supervisory, professional, or educational context. A linked source is not presented as validating the exact formula used by the application. In particular, the dashboard's loss, expense, combined, and underwriting-result formulas are application-defined calculations; the CAS source is conceptual context only. Claim frequency and severity are described in the SOA educational note, but the application still requires a valid supplied exposure measure to calculate frequency. Reinsurance retention and net retained loss remain methodology explanations only; no treaty engine is implemented.
